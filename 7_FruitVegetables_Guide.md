## 1. What This Mod Does

In vanilla, a vegetable keeps the same number of days wherever you put it, except in a fridge. A cabbage is stale in two days whether it sits on a sunny windowsill or in a cold cellar, and once the power is gone the fridge is just a cupboard.

This mod makes **where you keep vegetables matter**. A basement is a root cellar, an unheated room in winter is nearly as good, and potatoes, garlic and onions keep longer once they're cured. They're still the game's own vegetables, so every vanilla recipe takes them.

At harvest there's the **kraut barrel**: cabbages and turnips shredded with salt, pressed down under a stone and left to sour into Sauerkraut or Sauer Turnip that keeps the winter. Still to come: the **clamp**, a straw-and-earth mound for root crops in the field.

## 2. Quick Start

> **Requires Traditional Preservation (Core)**, the series' shared mod (TP Core for short). Enable it alongside this one; it loads first.

| Step | You need | You get |
|---|---|---|
| Harvest or find vegetables | Potatoes, turnips, sugar beets, carrots, cabbages, leeks, garlic, onions | |
| Leave potatoes a week in a mild room (10 to 20 C) | | **Cured** potatoes |
| Leave garlic and onions two weeks anywhere dry (not a basement) | | **Cured** garlic and onions |
| Put roots and cabbages in a crate in a **basement** | | Months of keeping instead of days |
| Build a **Kraut Barrel** | Hammer and saw (kept), 6 planks, 10 nails, 4 twine. Carpentry 3 | The barrel |
| **Pack Cabbage** and **Pack Turnip** | 20 to 60 heads, any mix; apples and carrots if you like | A packed barrel |
| **Salt and Compress** | A use of salt per 5 heads, a ripped sheet, a plank, a flat stone | It starts to sour |
| Wait **about 4 weeks** at 18 C | | **Sauerkraut**, a portion a head |

There's nothing to build for the cellar and no recipe to learn: the cellar is the place.

## 3. The Root Cellar

Every vegetable below keeps by the temperature of where it is. These are its **days fresh**; rotten follows at the game's own pace after that.

| Vegetable | Warm room, 22 C | Cool room, 15 C | Basement, 8 C | Powered fridge | Vanilla |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Potato | 28 | 60 | 150 | 140 | 28 |
| Potato, cured | 39 | 84 | **210** | 196 | |
| Turnip | 28 | 28 | 120 | 140 | 28 |
| Sugar Beet | 4 | 14 | 120 | 120 | 4 |
| Carrots | 6 | 14 | 120 | 120 | 6 |
| Cabbage | 7 | 14 | 90 | 90 | 2 |
| Leek | 5 | 10 | 60 | 60 | 5 |
| Garlic, Onion | 14 | 28 | 30 | 70 | 14 |
| Garlic, Onion, cured | 90 | **180** | 60 | onion 180, garlic 70 | |
| Sweet Potato | 28 | 120 | 30 | 140 | 28 |
| Sweet Potato, cured | 56 | **240** | 45 | 140 | |
| Pumpkin | 30 | 45 | 30 | 70 | 14 |
| Pumpkin, cured | 60 | **90** | 60 | 140 | |

- **Never worse than vanilla.** A vegetable in a warm room keeps vanilla's days (a cabbage a week: vanilla's two days are no real cabbage's); anywhere cooler keeps longer.
- **Colder than a fridge counts as a fridge.** Frost doesn't harm them here (yet).
- **A powered freezer stops the clock**, as in vanilla.
- **Garlic and onions hate the damp.** A basement is the wrong place for them: hang them in a dry room. Garlic sprouts in a fridge, too, but keeps best of all in a freezing-cold, dry room upstairs.
- **Sweet potatoes and pumpkins hate the cold.** Below about 10 C a sweet potato is chilled and a pumpkin rots in the damp: a cool room suits them better than the basement.
- **Cooked or burnt vegetables** go by the game's own clock.
- It keeps counting while you're away, by the place the vegetables were left in.

> *Margin note:* a root cellar works because the ground keeps its own season. A few feet down the soil barely feels a cold snap or a heat wave; it follows the average of the past month or so. Old farmhouses dug them under the kitchen, into a hillside, or as a lone mound with a door in the yard.

## 4. Curing

Curing isn't a recipe. It's the first part of keeping, done in the right place.

| Vegetable | Cures in | Where | What it gains |
|---|---|---|---|
| Potato | 7 days | Somewhere between 10 and 20 C: a room, a covered porch in autumn | Every day after counts about 70 percent of an uncured one's: 210 days in a basement instead of 150 |
| Garlic, Onion | 14 days under a roof or in a room with a window open in summer warmth (19 in the open; a shut room never); months in winter | Moving air and warmth; never a basement, a fridge or a freezer | 90 to 180 days in a dry room, 60 in a basement |
| Sweet Potato | 7 days | Somewhere warm, 20 to 32 C: by a fire in autumn | About 8 months at 15 C; still chilled in a basement |
| Pumpkin | 10 days | Somewhere warm, 20 to 32 C: by a fire in autumn | Twice as long everywhere: about 3 months at 15 C |

- **A potato cures by the temperature.** A winter basement (about 5 C) is too cold; a summer room above 20 C too warm. The days add up, so a potato moved in and out still gets there.
- **Garlic and onions cure in moving air and warmth.** A porch roof, an open shed or a room with its window open (usually upstairs: the ground floor is boarded up) in summer is best; a shut room doesn't cure them at all unless a fire is burning in it (a lit fire draws air through the room, so beside it they dry as on a porch; banked embers don't); the cold slows it right down (a fifth at 4 C and below); a basement never. Hang them at harvest, not in winter. While they cure somewhere dry they already keep by the cured days, so curing costs them nothing. Taken down to a basement before they're cured, they keep by the damp days until they come back up.
- **Sweet potatoes and pumpkins want it warm.** In June and July a room is warm enough. At the autumn harvest an unheated room has dropped under 20 C, so without power they won't cure there; with the grid on, a room's 22 C will do. Without power, do what growers did: set the crate within two tiles of a lit fireplace, antique oven or campfire (28 C, banked embers 24 C). A potato doesn't belong there: too warm for its cure, though it keeps no worse than in a warm room. The fire counts while the wood it had when you last looked lasts, so keep it fed and look in on the crate now and then.
- `TPFV.veg()` in the debug console shows whether something is cured (see the Toolkit).

### The onion and garlic pole and the crates

Three things to build that help, each a container you put vegetables in. The place still decides; these only add to it.

| Build | You need (hammer and saw kept) | Holds | What it does |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Onion and Garlic Pole**: a T-pole hung with bunches, for garlic and onions | Carpentry 2: 3 planks, 4 nails, 2 twine | 20 | Garlic and onions cure **half again as fast** (about 9 days under a roof, 16 in a room) and **keep a third longer** while they hang |
| **Vegetable Crate (Hay)**: an open crate lined with hay | Carpentry 1: 3 planks, 4 nails, 4 hay (from a farm's hay bales) | 35 | Potatoes, sweet potatoes and pumpkins cure **half again as fast** (a potato in about 5 days); potatoes, turnips, cabbages, sweet potatoes and pumpkins **keep a third longer**. Carrots, beets and leeks gain nothing here |
| **Vegetable Crate (Sand)**: roots packed in damp sand, their tops showing | Carpentry 1: 3 planks, 4 nails, 1 sack of sand | 35 | Carrots, sugar beets, leeks and turnips **keep a third longer**: the sand stops them drying out |

Each shows what it holds: bunches on the pole, roots and heads in the hay, rows of tops in the sand.

**Braids.** Select 2 to 10 garlic or onions and right-click: **Braid Garlic** or **Braid Onions**. A braid is only lighter to carry; the bulbs keep and cure inside it as loose ones do, and a braid hung on the Onion and Garlic Pole counts as hung there. **Unbraid** gives them back.

> *Margin note:* potatoes were cured in shallow bins or under sacking on a barn floor, in the dark, because light turns them green. Garlic and onions went the other way, up into the rafters in bunches or plaits where the air could move through them. A softneck garlic plait hung by the kitchen door all winter, and you cut a bulb off when you needed one.

> *Margin note:* a fresh potato's skin is thin and easily scuffed. Kept a week or two somewhere mild and damp, it grows a corky layer over every scrape, and that's what lets it sit in the dark until spring. Garlic and onions are the other way round: their necks have to dry out completely, or rot gets in through them.

## 5. The Cellarer's Almanac (Seasons & Places)

A basement follows the ground, a month behind the season: about 5 C from January to March and 13 C from July to September. An unheated room follows the last ten days of weather. (A room is 22 C while the grid is on.) So the best place changes through the year.

Days fresh for a vegetable put away on the first of the month, with no power, from a sampled Kentucky year:

| Put away | Cabbage, room | Cabbage, basement | Potato, room | Potato, basement |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| January | 90 | 90 | 134 | 143 |
| April | 42 | 65 | 84 | 123 |
| July | 9 | 20 | 43 | 99 |
| September | 12 | 19 | 123 | 140 |
| October | 25 | 24 | 174 | 169 |
| November | 88 | 57 | 140 | 191 |

- **At harvest the basement is still warm.** In October the ground hasn't cooled yet, so a cabbage keeps about three weeks either way. The long keeping starts as the ground cools in winter.
- **Potatoes picked in September cure on their own** in a mild room, then keep five or six months.
- **In midwinter a cold room is as good as a cellar**, and on a mild November day a room can be better than the still-warm basement.
- **Summer is the hungry gap.** Nothing keeps long in July: eat fresh, or ferment.

## 6. The Kraut Barrel

A standing oak barrel, narrower at the top, bound with iron: Fish & Meat's Salting Tub's cousin. One barrel, one batch: you put up most of the crop at once, salt and compress it, and leave it.

**Building it.** Build menu, Furniture: a hammer and a saw (kept), 6 planks, 10 nails and 4 twine, Carpentry 3. It holds about 50 litres.

**Packing.** Right-click the barrel:

| Row | Takes | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| **Pack Cabbage** / **Pack Turnip** | All (as many as you hold), or One | Cabbages and turnips mix. 20 heads at least, 60 at most (a bigger barrel waits the same weeks). Rotten heads won't do |
| **Add Apple** / **Add Carrot** | All, or One | Up to one of each for every 5 heads. Their food goes into the kraut |
| **Unpack the Barrel** | | Everything back. Only until it is salted and compressed. Packed but not sealed, they go on ageing as if left on the table: the barrel only keeps once it is sealed |
| **Salt and Compress** | A use of salt per 5 heads, a cloth (ripped sheet, burlap or cheesecloth), a plank, a flat stone | The salt goes in and the kraut is pressed down under the cloth, the board and the stone until its own juice rises over it. **Sealed**: nothing more goes in |

**Souring.** It works by the temperature where the barrel stands, like everything in the series:

| Kept at | Ready in |
|---|---|
| 0 C and below | Never: it waits |
| 4 C | 120 days |
| 8 C | 60 days |
| 13 C | 40 days |
| 18 C | 28 days |
| 21 C | 21 days |
| 24 C and up | 16 days |

- **Too warm spoils it.** Two days above 27 C before it's ready and it comes out **soft**: it keeps only two thirds as long. Five days and it **spoils**: empty it to get the plank and stone back. A barrel by a fire is too warm.
- **Kraut Barrel Info** (in its menu) shows the stage and when it will be ready, the place and its heat, what's in it, how far it has soured and the hours it has been too warm. Drag cabbages, turnips, apples or carrots onto the barrel in it to pack them; its buttons salt and compress, unpack, take kraut out (one button a kraut: One or All) and empty a spoiled barrel.

**Ready.** The kraut stays in the barrel under its stone and keeps by place: about six months in an 8 C cellar, three in a cool room, one in a warm one. **Take Sauerkraut** and **Take Sauer Turnip** (All or One): one portion a head, as its kind, each with its own food and the apples and carrots shared out. Out of the barrel a portion keeps two weeks, less if the barrel has stood long. The last portion gives the plank and stone back (the cloth is used up).

| Salted and compressed on the 1st | Room | Basement | Open air |
|---|---|---|---|
| August | ready in 27 days, keeps 220 | 39, keeps 154 | 28, keeps 238 |
| September | 33, keeps 238 | 41, keeps 185 | 40, keeps 227 |
| October | 167 (waits out most of the winter) | 47, keeps 196 | 183 |
| November | 182 | 60, keeps 181 | 182 |
| December | 164 | 83, keeps 154 | 157 |
| July | 22, keeps 46 | 40, keeps 123 | 21, soft: keeps 29 |

- **At harvest, sour it in the basement.** The basement is still about 13 C in October and sours it in seven weeks; a barrel put down there in midwinter takes two to three months. An unheated room in a Kentucky winter sits around 0 C, where the souring all but stops, so a barrel there waits for spring.
- **Pick its place before you pack it.** A barrel with anything in it can't be picked up, so build or put it down where it will sour and keep: a late-summer room for quick kraut, the basement for kraut that keeps.

> *Margin note:* on the Volga and all over Russia, *kvashenaya kapusta* was made in October in oak *kadki*, shredded cabbage trodden down with salt, carrot and sometimes whole apples, under a wooden circle and a river stone. The cabbage's own juice is the brine: the salt draws it out, and the lactic bacteria on every leaf do the rest. The stone keeps the kraut under the brine, away from the air and the moulds.

## 7. Secrets & Hidden Features

- **Loot counts too.** A sack of potatoes from a store keeps by place like your own harvest.
- **Open the window for garlic.** It cures by the window as well as on a porch, and a shut room won't cure it at all; once it's cured, shut the window again if you like.
- **A full barrel can be carried.** Its batch goes with it: start it upstairs in late summer, carry it down when it's ready.

---

## 8. Tips

- Cure potatoes in a mild room first, then carry them down to the basement.
- Hang garlic and onions upstairs, never in the basement.
- Cabbages and carrots want the coldest place you have: the basement in winter, a cold room in a cold snap.

## 9. Troubleshooting

| Problem | Fix |
|---|---|
| Vegetables still go off fast in a basement | Look at the month: the ground is still warm in late summer and early autumn (chapter 5). |
| Garlic in the basement goes off sooner than in a room | That's right: the basement is too damp for it. |
| Nothing changes at all | Check the sandbox: **Vegetables keep by place** must be on. |
| The barrel hasn't moved in weeks | At 0 C it stops, and near it it crawls. The Kraut Barrel window says so: move it somewhere warmer (the basement), or wait for spring. |
| Salt and Compress is red | Its hover says what's missing: 20 heads, the salt, a cloth, a plank or a flat stone. |
| I can't add more to the barrel | Once it is salted and compressed it is sealed, as a real barrel is. |

## 10. Sandbox Settings

| Setting | Default | What it does |
|---|---|---|
| Vegetables keep by place | On | Off, vegetables keep as vanilla's everywhere. |
| Kraut ferment (days at 18 C) | 28 | Days a kraut barrel takes at 18 C; the rest scale with it. |
| Temperature sensitivity | Normal | Off, Forgiving, Normal, Harsh. Off: every barrel takes the same time anywhere, and is never too warm. |

The game's own **Food Spoilage** setting applies on top, as for every food.
